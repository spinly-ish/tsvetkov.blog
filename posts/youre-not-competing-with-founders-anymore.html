You're Not Competing With Founders Anymore

Being a founder in 2026 feels a bit like running a zoo you definitely built but can't quite explain. You're technically a founder. In practice, you're a night-shift operator — Claude Code here, OpenClaw there, a hundred agents all "about to break everything." Some founders who hadn't touched code in ten years are back at their keyboards at 2am. Others with zero technical background are handing entire chunks of their business to agents and just hoping it holds. Nobody knows what "under control" means anymore. Probably because it doesn't mean anything anymore.

This is where the new anxiety kicks in. You used to compare yourself to the startup down the street. Now you're being compared to some guy who "built a startup on agents in two weeks." Read that tweet, then try to figure out why you slept last night. The race isn't human anymore — it's agentic. And your main competitor isn't another exhausted founder in a co-working space. It's a swarm of models that doesn't tire, doesn't doubt itself, and definitely doesn't spiral at 11pm after reading someone else's launch post.

You can't outrun a swarm by running faster. The new game isn't about doing more — it's about staying human in a world that's running on autopilot. Because in the end, the agents don't know why you're building or who you're building for. That part is still yours.
